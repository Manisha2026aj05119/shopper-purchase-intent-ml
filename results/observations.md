| ML Model Name | Observation about model performance |
|---|---|
| Logistic Regression | Cross-validation picked strong regularisation (C=0.1) with balanced class weights. Recall rose from 0.36 (defaults) to 0.74 and MCC from 0.459 to 0.519, at the cost of precision (0.50) and accuracy, which sits exactly at the 84.5 % majority rate. AUC 0.895 shows the linear boundary ranks sessions well; `PageValues` dominates the coefficients. A solid, fast and interpretable baseline, but the straight-line boundary limits it on this data. |
| Decision Tree | A shallow tree (max_depth=6, min_samples_leaf=20, balanced) is the strongest model: best AUC (0.914), best recall (0.82, only 67 of 382 purchases missed) and best MCC (0.570). The default unpruned tree overfits badly (AUC 0.736), so depth and leaf-size limits were essential. Splits are driven overwhelmingly by `PageValues` (importance 0.82), then a November visit, product-page duration and exit rate, which is easy to explain to a business user. |
| KNN | k=31 with distance weighting gives the highest accuracy (0.873) and precision (0.74) but the lowest recall (0.28): it flags only 145 sessions as purchases and misses 275 real ones. Distances in the 75-dimensional scaled + one-hot space are dominated by the many binary columns, so neighbourhoods are noisy and KNN has no class-weight option to correct the imbalance. Its AUC (0.847) is second-lowest, ahead only of Naive Bayes, and tuning for AUC actually lowered its F1 (0.406 vs 0.490 with defaults). |
| Naive Bayes | Gaussian NB is the weakest model (AUC 0.807, MCC 0.384). Its conditional-independence assumption is violated (page counts and durations are strongly correlated) and a Gaussian is a poor fit for zero-inflated, right-skewed features and for one-hot columns. With the default var_smoothing it collapsed to predicting 'purchase' almost everywhere (accuracy 0.27, recall 0.97); var_smoothing=0.1 repairs it to a usable but still inferior model. Its one advantage is speed. |
| Support Vector Machine | The RBF SVM (C=4.0, gamma=0.01, balanced) is a close second: best F1 (0.635), MCC 0.565 and AUC 0.902, with the best precision/recall balance among the recall-oriented models (0.55 / 0.75). Balanced weights lifted recall from 0.47 to 0.75. It is the most expensive model to tune and to score, and it offers no direct interpretability. |
| **Overall, Winner for your dataset?** | **Decision Tree** (tuned). It leads on the three metrics that matter under a 15 % positive rate: AUC 0.914, recall 0.82 and MCC 0.570, with F1 0.633 within 0.002 of the best. The Support Vector Machine is a close second (MCC 0.565) but costs far more to train and is a black box, while the tree is the fastest model and its splits can be read directly. Accuracy would have crowned KNN, which misses 275 of 382 purchases, a reminder that accuracy is the wrong yardstick on imbalanced data. |
